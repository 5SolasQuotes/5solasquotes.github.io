13 quotations · Works cited: At the Reformation Anabaptists, and later Quakers and many Independants, argued that, as the Levitical statutes are done away with, so no law remains to bind persons to systematic giving to the Christian ministry, and that ministers need not, and should not be paid. However, even before the Law with Moses, being yet a gentile, Abraham paid a tithe to the priest Melchizedek (Gen. 14:18-20; Heb. 7:4-9) and Jacob vowed to give a tenth unto God with all that He prospered him with (Gen. 28:20-22).
